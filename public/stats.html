Winrate — here, in the by-day table and on the cards at the top — runs low to high: under 30%, 30-50%, 50-70%, 70-90%, 90%+, grey = fewer than 8 games, which is too few to mean anything. ⚠ Green is high, not correct: the ladder aims each level at its own target, so a green level well above target is a level that got easier than it was meant to be.

All three winrate columns are completed ÷ started, so an attempt someone walked away from counts against the level — it used to be counted over finished attempts only, which quietly deleted every abandonment from the maths and flattered exactly the levels people were giving up on. They differ in who is counted: Winrate/game = every attempt begun. Winrate/player = players who ever cleared it ÷ players who ever started it. Winrate_c = the same as /game but counting only the players who cleared it in the end, so it is roughly how many goes it takes someone who can do it: 50% is two attempts, 33% is three. ⚠ It is not a success rate — by construction everyone in it succeeded — and it can only fall as people retry. ⚠ Read together they separate two different faults. Level 10 currently reads 48% / 85% / 73%: most attempts end in failure, yet nearly everyone gets there, taking about one and a half goes. A level where all three sag is a different problem from one where only the first does.

⚠ Only attempts the log can pair are counted — an end row whose start row is missing is dropped from both sides. Builds before start-logging sent no start rows, so their games are absent from every winrate here (they still count in Games, Completed and Starts (user)). Dividing raw wins by raw starts instead would put level 1 at 169%. Starts (event) = how many times this board was entered; a player who retried it five times is five. Starts (user) = how many distinct people those were, counted once each. Both include people who arrived and walked away without finishing — that is what makes them the pair to read Quit against. ⚠ Starts (event) shows —, not 0, for levels whose only rows come from builds too old to send a start row; Starts (user) still counts those people, because finishing an attempt here proves they started one. ⚠ So the two are not on the same footing while old rows are in the window, and event can come out lower than user — level 1 currently reads 318 events against 561 people, because a few hundred of them played on a build that sent no start row. Dividing one by the other is only meaningful once the whole window is start-logging builds; until then a ratio under 1 is a gap in the data, not players who somehow started less than once. Completed = how many of those players ever cleared it. Quit = attempts that reached the board and never came back with a result — someone who walked away mid-level. ⚠ It is a percentage of Starts (event), not of Starts (user). Won & left = players who cleared this level and were never seen at the next one. They did not leave because it was too hard, so this is the churn difficulty cannot explain. ⚠ Read the last few rows with suspicion: the newest level in the window has no next level to be absent from, and someone who cleared a board minutes ago has not had time to start the one after it — both push this number up on their own. Drop-off = players who started this level and never started the next one. Peak belt is how full the conveyor ever got, averaged — the thing stars are scored on, so a level near 100% is one nobody has slack on.
